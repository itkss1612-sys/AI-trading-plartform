Central Pivot Range (CPR) Master Framework

The Central Pivot Range (CPR) is the single most widely used intraday framework by Indian professional traders. It uses the previous session's High, Low, and Close to project critical institutional support and resistance for today.

Pivot (P) = (High + Low + Close) / 3
Bottom Central (BC) = (High + Low) / 2
Top Central (TC) = (Pivot - BC) + Pivot
Resistance 1 (R1) = 2 * Pivot - Low
Support 1 (S1) = 2 * Pivot - High

How to Trade CPR Width:

  • Narrow CPR (Width < 0.25%): Signals consolidation in the prior session. Indicates high probability of a strong trending breakout day. Look for aggressive continuation trades beyond R1 or S1.
  • Wide CPR (Width > 0.40%): Indicates a large trend day already occurred yesterday. Expect a range-bound, sideways session. Avoid breakout chasing; fade extremes.

Trend Indicators (SMA 20, SMA 50, Supertrend):

  • SMA 20 (Cyan Line): Short-term trend momentum. In a healthy uptrend, price bounces off the 20 SMA.
  • SMA 50 (Amber Line): Intermediate trend filter. When price is above both 20 & 50 SMA, take strictly LONG trades.
  • Supertrend (10, 3): Green band below price denotes bullish institutional support; Red band above denotes bearish supply.

Smart Money Concepts (SMC)

Smart Money Concepts identify where institutional algorithms and banks enter the market rather than relying on lagging retail indicators.

1. Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is a 3-candle price imbalance created by aggressive institutional displacement.

  • Bullish FVG (Green Highlight Box): Candle 3's Low is strictly above Candle 1's High, leaving an unfilled price void. When price pulls back into this box, institutions often reload buy orders.
  • Bearish FVG (Red Highlight Box): Candle 3's High is strictly below Candle 1's Low.
  • UNMITIGATED Status: Price has not yet retested this zone. This represents the highest probability reaction area.

2. High-Conviction Candlestick Patterns

  • Bullish Engulfing: Large green candle body completely engulfs prior red candle body. Signals demand dominance. Enter on confirmation close above the engulfing bar.
  • Hammer (Pinbar): Long lower wick ($2\times$ body length) showing violent price rejection from lows by aggressive buyers.
  • Shooting Star: Long upper wick at resistance showing aggressive institutional selling.

Trade Risk Planner & Position Sizing

The secret to long-term profitability is never risking more than 1.0% to 1.5% of your total capital on any single trade.

Allowed Capital Risk (₹) = Account Capital * (Risk % / 100)
Position Quantity = Allowed Capital Risk / (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price)
Reward-to-Risk (R:R) = (Target - Entry) / (Entry - Stop Loss)
Minimum 1.5 : 1 R:R Rule: Never take a trade with a reward-to-risk ratio below 1.5:1. Even with a 50% win rate, a 2:1 R:R guarantees positive mathematical expectancy.

Indian Statutory Regulatory Charges Breakdown:

  • Brokerage: Flat ₹20 per executed order (or 0.03%, whichever is lower).
  • STT / CTT (Securities Transaction Tax): 0.025% on sell side (Equity Intraday) / 0.1% (Delivery).
  • Exchange Turnover Fee: 0.00325% on turnover (NSE).
  • SEBI Turnover Fee: ₹10 per crore (0.0001%).
  • Stamp Duty: 0.003% on buy side (Intraday).
  • GST: 18% on (Brokerage + Exchange turnover + SEBI fees).
  • Breakeven Points: Exact points per share needed to pay all fees.

Options Chain & Put-Call Ratio (PCR):

  • Put-Call Ratio (PCR) = Total Put OI / Total Call OI
  • PCR > 1.2 (Bullish): Put writers dominate, creating strong floor support under the market.
  • PCR < 0.8 (Bearish): Call writers dominate, creating overhead resistance.
  • Delta ($\Delta$): Sensitivity of option price to underlying move. ATM Call delta $\approx 0.50$.
